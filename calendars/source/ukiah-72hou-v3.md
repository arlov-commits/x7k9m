# 優凱谷七十二候 — Ukiah Valley 72 Hou

**Locale:** Ukiah Valley / City of Ten Thousand Buddhas, 95482
**Frame:** tropical solar longitude, 5° per 候, 3 候 per 節氣
**Version:** 3.0 — restructured for phase, rebirded, defished, glossed

---

## What makes a 候 feel like a turning

You were right that v2 read as snapshots. But I don't think 漸 is the fix — I think 漸 is what you reach for when you can't find the actual device. The classical list has four, and I'd only used one and a half of them.

**1. The 候 is a synecdoche for the whole period.** 東風解凍 does not mean "on February 4th the east wind melts ice." It means: *thawing is what the world is now doing*, and here is one visible handle on it. The image has to be chosen so that it implies a world-state. A fact that only reports itself will always read as a snapshot no matter how you phrase it. So this version gives each 節氣 a **phase line** and treats its three 候 as beats of that phase rather than three independent observations.

**2. Aspectual staging, not gradualness.** The classical particles are 始 (begins), 乃 (thereupon, and so), 咸 (all together), 先 (ahead of the others), 忽 (abruptly), 時 (in its season). These mark *where in a process you are* — which is what 漸 was groping at, but 漸 flattens the process into a slope, and a slope has no moment you can stand in. 乃 is the most useful of them: it carries consequence. 禾乃登, "and so the grain comes in" — because of everything before it. This version uses 乃 nine times and 漸 zero.

**3. Threads.** Subjects recur at intervals, and the recurrence *is* the phenology: 蟄蟲始振 → 蟄蟲坯戶 → 蟄蟲咸俯; 鴻雁來 → 鴻雁來賓; 桃始華 → 靡草死. You feel the year turn because you keep meeting the same creature in a different condition. v2 had a few of these by accident. v3 has fourteen on purpose, listed at the end.

**4. Absence as event.** 反舌無聲 — the mockingbird stops singing. 鶡鴠不鳴. 虹藏不見. The world *subtracting* something is one of the strongest turning-markers available, and v2 had none. This version has three, deliberately matching the classical count.

Also: the classical list is about **28% birds** — twenty of seventy-two. v2 was at eleven. This version is at eighteen, including your geese.

Fish are gone. Both steelhead entries removed; they were accurate about the hatchery and useless about what you can see.

## The structural inversion

Ukiah is close to the inverse of the North China climate, and the calendar states it rather than smoothing it:

| | North China | Ukiah Valley |
|---|---|---|
| Green | spring–summer | **November–May** |
| Gold / dead | autumn | **June–October** |
| Dormancy | winter | **summer** |
| Deep flowering | 春分–立夏 | **冬至–大寒** |

The four hinge points are placed to land on the solar terms that carry the opposite meaning in Chinese — 夏至 as the death of the grass, 立冬 as the greening, 冬至 as the nest, 大暑 as the sleep.

# The calendar

Each term gives its phase, then three 候. The italic line under each 候 is what you would actually see; skip it once you know them.

---

### 立春 · 315–329° · early–mid February
*The wet season at its full depth. Nothing is beginning; things are being **put down** — an antler, a voice, a leaf — and the year opens by relinquishing.*

**315 · 鹿角解** — The buck lets go his antlers
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Blacktail bucks drop both antlers over a few days in Jan–Feb. You find them, or you notice the bucks are suddenly plain.*

**320 · 蛙聲徹夜** — Frog voice all night through
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Sierran treefrogs at full chorus in every ditch and seasonal pool. Loud enough to be a nuisance. The year's loudest sound.*

**325 · 七葉先萌** — The buckeye leafs out first, alone
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*California buckeye breaks bud in February while every other deciduous tree is bare. Pale apple-green fingers on grey wood. It runs its whole year early and quits in July.*

---

### 雨水 · 330–344° · mid–late February
*Water is the whole subject. It fills the ground, it collects the birds, and the first thing you can eat comes up out of it.*

**330 · 川壑皆盈** — Every ravine runs full
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Drainages that were dry stone in September are now audible from a distance. The valley has a background sound it lacks half the year.*

**335 · 雁集水田** — The geese gather on the flooded field
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Canada geese massed on standing water in winter pasture, along the river, at the lake. Noisy, conspicuous, and the first beat of the goose thread.*

**340 · 貫葉菜秀** — The pierced-leaf herb comes into its own
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Miner's lettuce: a round disc of a leaf with the stem running clean through the middle and a little spray of white flowers standing out of it. Shaded banks and the north side of oaks. Edible, mild, faintly sour.*

---

### 驚蟄 · 345–359° · early–mid March
*The phase of display. Everything that owns an ornament unfurls it — and the redbud does it before it even has leaves.*

**345 · 紫荊先花** — Redbud flowers ahead of its leaves
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Western redbud goes magenta on completely bare wood. Ornament before substance — the same move as 桃始華, in the same slot.*

**350 · 孔雀開屏** — The peacock raises his screen
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Display season opens and runs to high summer: the fan, the rattling shiver of the quills, and the screaming at all hours.*

**355 · 火雞曳翼** — The turkey drags his wings
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Wild toms in strut — tail fanned, primaries scraping the dirt, breast puffed, gobbling. They walk stiff-legged in slow circles around indifferent hens.*

---

### 春分 · 0–14° · late March
*Arrival and construction. The travellers land, and immediately begin building. The oaks release at the same moment, so the air itself is full of material.*

**0 · 玄鳥至** — The swallows arrive
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Barn and cliff swallows back overhead after six months gone. Kept verbatim from the Chinese — it lands in the same slot here.*

**5 · 燕銜泥** — The swallow carries mud
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Birds working a puddle edge, flying beakfuls up under the eaves. The gourd nests go up in about a week.*

**10 · 橡花垂穗** — The oaks hang out their tassels
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Catkins in dangling strings, then pollen on every horizontal surface — windshields, water, laundry. A yellow film over everything.*

---

### 清明 · 15–29° · early–mid April
*The exchange between flower and insect opens. Nothing is scarce.*

**15 · 藍眼遍草** — Blue eyes scattered through the grass
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Blue-eyed grass: small six-petalled violet-blue stars on grassy slopes, each with a yellow centre. In numbers they look like the field is looking back.*

**20 · 金燈懸莖** — Golden lanterns hang from the stem
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Diogenes' lanterns (golden globe lily) — pendant, translucent yellow globes on thin stalks in oak shade. Lit from inside when the sun is behind them.*

**25 · 蜂蝶咸出** — Bees and butterflies, all of them at once
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*咸 is the classical "all together." There is a week where the insect load goes from noticeable to total.*

---

### 穀雨 · 30–44° · late April
*What was hidden or unborn now walks in the open. The phase moves from tenderness to caution in three beats.*

**30 · 雁引雛行** — The geese lead their goslings in a line
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*One parent in front, one behind, the yellow-grey goslings strung between them, the whole file crossing a road or lawn at its own pace, refusing to hurry. Everything stops for them.*

**35 · 石龍拜日** — The rock-dragon bows to the sun
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Male fence lizards on a rock or post doing rapid push-ups, flashing the blue belly at rivals. It looks exactly like bowing, which is why the entry exists.*

**40 · 響尾出穴** — The rattle-tail leaves its hole
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Rattlesnakes out and moving. Watch the trail edges and the warm stone. The classical 蚯蚓出 with the stakes raised.*

---

### 立夏 · 45–59° · early–mid May
*The wet season's last flowering, and its voice ends. The frogs go quiet under the buckeye's candles.*

**45 · 七葉舉燭** — The buckeye lifts its candles
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Upright white flower spikes, eight inches or more, standing off the branches. Heavily scented, covered in insects. The tree's whole year is spent here.*

**50 · 鶉引雛** — The quail leads her chicks
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*California quail broods — a dozen chicks the size of bumblebees rolling across a path in a loose ball, the cock on a fencepost keeping watch.*

**55 · 蛙聲乃寂** — And so the frog voice falls silent
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*The pools go down and the chorus stops. An absence-候, closing the thread that opened in January. You notice it at night, about a week after it happens.*

---

### 小滿 · 60–74° · late May
*Handover. The winter voice has ended; within three weeks the summer voice replaces it, and everything green finishes its business.*

**60 · 芒穗乃堅** — And so the awned heads harden
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Foxtails go from soft green to barbed and brittle. They start getting into socks, and into dogs' ears and paws. The grass has stopped being food and become armament.*

**65 · 鹿子初生** — Fawns are born
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Spotted blacktail fawns, wobbly, usually two. Left folded in tall grass while the doe feeds elsewhere.*

**70 · 蜩始鳴** — The cicada begins to call
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*First scattered dry buzzing from the oaks — one insect, then a few. Opening the thread the frogs just closed.*

---

### 芒種 · 75–89° · early–mid June
*The peak of abundance, and the first failure. The nests are full at the same moment the water begins to come apart.*

**75 · 燕雛滿簷** — The eaves are full of swallow chicks
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Gaping heads crowding the mouths of the mud nests, adults shuttling constantly, the noise under a roofline all day.*

**80 · 乳草乃華** — And so the milkweed flowers
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Narrowleaf milkweed opens its dull-pink umbels. Monarchs come to it to lay; look for the striped caterpillars a few weeks on.*

**85 · 溪斷成潭** — The creek breaks into pools
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Continuous flow fails and the creek becomes a chain of disconnected pools with dry stone between them. The background sound of 雨水 ends here.*

---

### 夏至 · 90–104° · late June
*Heat becomes the medium. It is visible in the ground, it holds a bird up, and it has a sound. This is the term where the Chinese year is fullest and the Ukiah year dies.*

**90 · 草化黃金** — The grasses turn to gold
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Over roughly ten days the whole valley changes colour and the annual grasses finish. 化 is the transformation-verb from 腐草為螢, used deliberately: it does not look like drying, it looks like the hills becoming a different substance.*

**95 · 禿鷲乘熱** — The vulture rides the risen heat
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Turkey vultures on thermals over the gold hillsides, wings in a shallow V, rocking, not beating. Heat made visible by what it carries.*

**100 · 蜩鳴日中** — The cicadas call at midday
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Full chorus, loudest in the hottest hour, cutting out when a cloud crosses. The sound is functionally the sound of the temperature.*

---

### 小暑 · 105–119° · early–mid July
*The shedding phase. Everything sheds whatever it cannot afford — leaves, daylight, skin. This is the Ukiah year's austerity, and it is chosen, not suffered.*

**105 · 七葉先凋** — The buckeye withers first, alone
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Buckeye leaves go brown and crisp and drop in July, sometimes with the flowers still on the tree, while everything around it is green. It is not dying. It finished early.*

**110 · 地鼠夏蟄** — The ground squirrels take their summer sleep
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*California ground squirrel activity drops off sharply in the heat; adults go below and stay there. China's 蟄蟲 sleep in winter. Here the retreat is from the sun.*

**115 · 莓樹褪皮** — The madrone sheds its skin
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Pacific madrone peels its outer bark in thin red curls, leaving smooth cool green underneath. The tree looks skinned and much better for it.*

---

### 大暑 · 120–134° · late July
*At the extreme of dryness the last ornament falls, only the harshest thing still flowers — and the fruit that formed in secret becomes visible.*

**120 · 孔雀解翎** — The peacock sheds his train
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*The whole train comes out over a few weeks after breeding ends. The birds go abruptly plain and awkward, and there are eye-feathers on the ground everywhere. Same verb as 鹿角解, six months on.*

**125 · 星薊獨榮** — The star-thistle alone flourishes
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Yellow star-thistle in full spiny bloom across dead gold ground, the only thing blooming and the only thing feeding bees. Hated, invasive, and honestly the sole flower of this phase.*

**130 · 橡子乃見** — And so the acorns become visible
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Green acorns appear in the oaks, small and hard in their cups. They have been forming since the catkins in March; now you can see them.*

---

### 立秋 · 135–149° · early–mid August
*The night turns first, and the day does not turn at all. This is the term where Ukiah is two seasons at once.*

**135 · 涼風夜至** — The cool wind comes by night
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Ninety-five degrees at four in the afternoon; low fifties before dawn. The classical 涼風至, corrected for the fact that here it only arrives after dark.*

**140 · 白露降** — White dew descends
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Dew back on the grass at first light after two rainless months. Kept verbatim and in its true Chinese slot — 立秋 二候, not the solar term named 白露.*

**145 · 膠菊乃香** — And so the tarweed gives off its scent
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Tarweed opens yellow daisies in the dead grass and the whole field smells resinous, sticky and strange — closer to turpentine than to flowers. It is on your hands if you walk through it. The only smell-候 in the calendar.*

---

### 處暑 · 150–164° · late August
*Subtraction. The water ends, then the competition sharpens over what is left, then the sound ends.*

**150 · 溪涸見石** — The creek dries, showing its stones
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*The pools are gone and the bed is bare rock. The classical 水始涸, forty days early and much more absolute.*

**155 · 胡蜂爭食** — Yellowjackets contend for food
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Colonies at maximum, natural forage exhausted, so they come to whatever you are eating outdoors and get bold about it.*

**160 · 蜩聲忽止** — The cicadas abruptly stop
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Not a fade — an ending, over a few days. The absence-候 that closes the thread begun at 70. Suddenly you can hear the wind in the oaks again.*

---

### 白露 · 165–179° · early–mid September
*The world divides into those who leave and those who lay up.*

**165 · 蛛網懸露** — Webs hang weighted with dew
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Big orb-weaver webs across paths and between fence posts, invisible until the dew loads them, at which point they are everywhere and you have already walked into one.*

**170 · 玄鳥歸** — The swallows go home
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*They gather on wires for a few days and then the eaves are silent. Closes the swallow thread that opened at 春分.*

**175 · 松鼠藏橡** — The squirrel hides acorns
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Western gray squirrels carrying single acorns off and burying them one at a time, in a hundred places, most of which they will not find again. That is how oaks travel.*

---

### 秋分 · 180–194° · late September
*Storing against loss — and one creature who spends everything instead.*

**180 · 橡實落地** — Acorns fall
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*They come down hard on roofs and cars, and roll. Deer, jays, woodpeckers, squirrels and pigs all converge.*

**185 · 地蛛出穴** — The ground-spider leaves its burrow
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Male tarantulas walking the open hills at dusk after the first rain, looking for females, never returning. They are large, slow, harmless, and visibly on a one-way trip. China puts 蟄蟲坯戶 — hibernating insects seal their doors — in exactly this 5°.*

**190 · 啄木貯倉** — The woodpecker stocks his granary
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Acorn woodpeckers hammering acorns one-per-hole into a granary snag, thousands of holes, the whole clan working it and fighting over it loudly.*

---

### 寒露 · 195–209° · early–mid October
*The year's guests arrive with the rain, and the hillsides answer in colour.*

**195 · 初雨土香** — First rain, and the earth's scent
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*The smell that comes off dry ground in the first ten minutes of the first real rain. In a place with a five-month drought this is a genuine annual event, and people mention it to each other.*

**200 · 毒漆乃丹** — And so poison oak turns cinnabar
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*This is Ukiah's autumn colour — scarlet running up the hillsides and into the oaks, and the most beautiful thing in the landscape is the one you must not touch.*

**205 · 冠雀來賓** — The crowned sparrows come as guests
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Golden-crowned and white-crowned sparrows arrive to winter. The golden-crowned sings a thin descending three-note whistle all season. 來賓 — the ones who come and stay, from 鴻雁來賓.*

---

### 霜降 · 210–224° · late October
*Warmth is sought, the dead convert into the living, and the large animals begin their contest.*

**210 · 朱蝽附壁** — The vermilion bugs cling to the wall
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Boxelder bugs — black with red lines, often coupled tail-to-tail — massing on warm south and west walls before overwintering, then getting into the house. Your butt-to-butt bugs. Classical parallel: 蟋蟀居壁.*

**215 · 腐草生菌** — Rotted grass gives birth to mushrooms
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*First flush after the October rain, straight out of the dead thatch. Transposed from 腐草為螢 — there are no fireflies here, so the rotted grass becomes fungus instead.*

**220 · 鹿始交** — The deer begin to mate
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Blacktail rut. Bucks with swollen necks, chasing does across roads at bad hours, careless in a way they are not the rest of the year. Antlers now, gone by 立春.*

---

### 立冬 · 225–239° · early–mid November
*The single largest turning in the calendar: the ground goes green in the same fortnight the canopy goes bare. Growth and dormancy trade places.*

**225 · 秋草反青** — The autumn grasses turn green again
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Roughly two weeks after the first soaking rain, new grass comes through the dead gold and the valley reverses colour. The growing season is starting. China puts 水始冰 here.*

**230 · 白橡葉落** — The valley oak drops its leaves
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*The deciduous oaks let go while the ground underneath them turns green — the two events are simultaneous and worth standing still for.*

**235 · 蠑螈赴水** — The newts go down to the water
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*On rainy nights newts walk overland, slowly and in numbers, downhill to their breeding pools. Orange-bellied, unhurried, and they cross roads.*

---

### 小雪 · 240–254° · late November
*In the cold, food appears — and is taken.*

**240 · 熊果始華** — Manzanita begins to flower
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Small white-to-pink urns hanging in clusters on that red peeling wood, opening in November. The 桃始華 construction landing in late autumn.*

**245 · 石楠子丹** — Toyon berries redden
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Heavy clusters of scarlet berries on evergreen shrubs. The reason it is called Christmas berry, and the reason Hollywood is called that.*

**250 · 連雀降實** — The waxwings descend on the fruit
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*A flock of cedar waxwings arrives without warning, silent except for a high thin trilling, strips a toyon in a day, and is gone. Sleek, crested, faintly unreal. Constructed after 戴勝降于桑.*

---

### 大雪 · 255–269° · early–mid December
*The world seals, and the enclosure fills with sound and with growth from below.*

**255 · 谷霧不開** — The valley fog does not lift
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Tule fog settles on the valley floor and stays all day, sometimes for days. The hills above are in sun. It is cold, still and very quiet inside it.*

**260 · 菌乃遍林** — And so mushrooms fill the woods
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Peak fungal season. Boletes, russulas, coral fungi, things on logs. The forest floor is doing more visible work now than in June.*

**265 · 蜂鳥墜空** — The hummingbird falls out of the sky
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Male Anna's courtship dive: he climbs a hundred feet, drops nearly vertically, and pulls out with a loud explosive pop made by his tail feathers. In December.*

---

### 冬至 · 270–284° · late December
*The nadir of the light and the beginning of the nesting year. The tree is empty and the bird is building.*

**270 · 蜂鳥營巢** — The hummingbird builds her nest
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*A walnut-sized cup of plant down bound with spider silk and camouflaged with lichen, built at the winter solstice and timed to the manzanita bloom. China's 冬至 is 蚯蚓結, 麋角解 — the year's deepest stillness.*

**275 · 白橡枝空** — The valley oak's branches stand empty
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Full winter architecture, the whole crown legible for the only time all year. An absence-候, after 虹藏不見.*

**280 · 蚯蚓出** — Earthworms come out
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Up through the wet soil and out onto paths after rain. Same three characters as the Chinese 立夏 候 — here they belong to midwinter.*

---

### 小寒 · 285–299° · early–mid January
*In the deepest cold, calling begins. Two voices open the year from opposite ends of the valley.*

**285 · 鴞乃相答** — And so the owls answer one another
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Great horned owls duetting at dusk and before dawn — the male lower, the female higher and faster, back and forth. They are pair-bonding to nest in February. Compare 鶡鴠不鳴, the bird that stops singing at China's 大雪.*

**290 · 霜聚下田** — Frost gathers in the low fields
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Cold air drains downhill overnight, so the valley floor is white and the slopes above it are not. The frost has a visible upper edge.*

**295 · 蛙始鳴** — The frogs begin to call
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*A few voices from the wet ground, tentative and intermittent. By 立春 it will be the loudest thing in the valley.*

---

### 大寒 · 300–314° · late January
*The coldest term is the flowering term. Everything about this is backwards and it is the best month in the year to walk.*

**300 · 熊果花盛** — Manzanita in full flower
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Whole shrubs hung with pink-white urns, audibly busy with bees and hummingbirds, in January.*

**305 · 丹蒿出土** — A scarlet spike breaks the leaf litter
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Indian warrior: a dense crimson-purple flower head pushing straight up out of the oak duff, with fern-like reddish leaves. It looks like it came up in the wrong century.*

**310 · 雙鷹盤空** — A pair of hawks turns in the sky
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Red-tailed hawks in courtship flight — two birds circling together, high, sometimes locking talons and tumbling, screaming. Leads directly into 立春.*

# Threads

These are the point. Read down a thread and the year moves; read across the table and it doesn't.

| Thread | Beats |
|---|---|
| **Swallow** | 玄鳥至 (0) → 燕銜泥 (5) → 燕雛滿簷 (75) → 玄鳥歸 (170) |

| **Buckeye** | 七葉先萌 (325) → 七葉舉燭 (45) → 七葉先凋 (105) |

The frog and cicada threads are the ones to notice: they hand off to each other twice a year, at 立夏/小滿 and at 處暑/白露. The valley is never without one of those two sounds, and it never has both.

# Naming notes

**Standard, used as-is:** 紫荊 Cercis · 熊果 Arctostaphylos · 藍眼草 Sisyrinchium · 石楠 Heteromeles (加州石楠) · 蠑螈 newt · 蜂鳥 hummingbird · 火雞 turkey · 胡蜂 yellowjacket · 禿鷲 turkey vulture · 鴞 owl · 孔雀 · 蜩 · 鶉 · 雁

**Coined or shortened — flagged so nothing passes as established:**

- **七葉** — *Aesculus californica*, buckeye. From 七葉樹, cut to two characters.
- **莓樹** — *Arbutus menziesii*, madrone. From 草莓樹 (properly the European strawberry tree).
- **石龍** — *Sceloporus occidentalis*, western fence lizard. Borrowed from 石龍子, which is a skink and the wrong family. "Rock dragon" was too good for the bowing display to give up. A knowing appropriation, not an identification.
- **響尾** — rattlesnake, from 響尾蛇.
- **地蛛** — tarantula. Technical Chinese is 捕鳥蛛, "bird-catching spider," which misdescribes it. 地蛛, ground-spider, is what it is.
- **朱蝽** — *Boisea rubrolineata*, western boxelder bug. No good Chinese common name exists; 朱 for the red lines, 蝽 for the true-bug family.
- **膠菊** — *Madia elegans*, tarweed, from 黏膠菊.
- **貫葉菜** — *Claytonia perfoliata*, miner's lettuce. "Pierced-leaf herb," for the disc the stem runs through. Entirely my coinage.
- **星薊** — yellow star-thistle, from 黃星薊.
- **乳草** — *Asclepias fascicularis*, narrowleaf milkweed. Standard is 馬利筋; 乳草 is transparent.
- **丹蒿** — *Pedicularis densiflora*, Indian warrior. Genus is 馬先蒿; 丹 for the crimson.
- **白橡** — *Quercus lobata*, valley oak, the deciduous one — as against the evergreen 橡 of 橡實落地.
- **地鼠** — *Otospermophilus beecheyi*, California ground squirrel. Distinct from 松鼠, the western gray squirrel of 松鼠藏橡.
- **連雀** — cedar waxwing. Standard modern Chinese is 太平鳥; 連雀 is the older/Japanese form and scans better.
- **冠雀** — *Zonotrichia* sparrows, golden- and white-crowned. Coined for the crown.
